The Keeper Tax

Chad put a proposal up and it cleared the bar to be voted on. Ryan, Phil and Jeremy all said the same thing back: make it hit sooner and harder. Here are two versions built from that, plus the one thing about the original that needs saying out loud first.
Read this part first

Under the rule we have right now, a keeper gets more expensive on his own. First year you keep him he costs the round you drafted him. Every year after that he costs his current expert consensus rank. Get good, get expensive.

The proposal as written removes that. Three straight years at the original draft cost, and the only thing that ever goes up is a cash fee starting in year four at ten dollars. That is not a tax on elite keepers. That is a discount on them.

What it looks like in practice. Jaxon Smith-Njigba was a 7th round pick. He is now the 5th best player in football. Under today's rule he costs a 1st. Under the proposal as written he costs a 7th this year, a 7th next year, and the entire penalty for the third year is ten dollars. That is Phil's point exactly, and he is right.

What other leagues do

Both versions below come out of how keeper leagues actually solve this.
The common answer

In snake leagues the standard fix is round escalation — the keeper costs a better pick every year you hold him. It is described as intuitive and fair, and it creates a real decision at every round level instead of only at the top.

Money is the auction answer

Escalating dollar fees are the mechanism auction leagues use, because in an auction the price is already money. We draft snake. Our currency is picks.

The middle path

The most widely used setup is a three year cap paired with escalating cost. Both matter. Without one of them elite players never re-enter the pool at fair value, which is the exact failure Phil named.

The two versions

Pick one. Both keep everything else the same — 4 keepers, max 3 WR, max 2 discounts, existing luxury tax on bumps, traded players carry their status, only a trip through free agency resets a player.

  • Early-round keepers burn out fast. A 1st or 2nd is a two year hold.

What actually separates them

One question, and it is not really about money.

Version A says: if you found him and you are willing to pay for him, he is yours as long as you want him. The league gets paid instead.

Version B says: nobody owns a player forever. Every star goes back in the pool eventually and everyone gets a shot at him.

Both are defensible. They are just different leagues. Vote for the one you would rather play in.

Still open either way: what counts as a consecutive year when a player is traded mid-season, and whether the Player Tax stacks on top of the existing luxury tax for a bumped keeper. Chad's draft says traded players carry their status and only free agency resets the clock — that part is clean and both versions keep it.

Threshold: a rule change needs 8 of 12 yes votes. This one already has enough interest to go to a vote, so what is left is agreeing on which version is on the ballot.
