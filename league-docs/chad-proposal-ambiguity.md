# The unresolved contradiction in Chad's Player Tax proposal

## Reading 1 — the formal proposal says the discount lasts THREE years
His opening line: "A player may be kept for up to three seasons at their original draft cost/ADP."
  Year 2 — First Keeper Year: "keep the player at their original draft position/ADP"
  Year 3 — Second Keeper Year: "keep the player for a third consecutive season at their
           original draft position/ADP"
And his table lists the Keeper Cost column as "Original draft position" for EVERY year, 1 through 8.

Under this reading the discount never expires. A 7th-round pick costs a 7th forever, and the
only thing that ever changes is the cash fee. That is a MAJOR change to how keepers are priced,
and it is a loosening, not a tightening.

## Reading 2 — his follow-up says year 3 reverts to ADP
At 11:59, answering Jeremy's "get to the part where you pay extra faster":
  "I thought about that but then it kind of loses value of a keeper.
   Year 1 drafted
   Year 2 keep in draft spot/adp
   Year 3 ADP
   Year 4 pay"

Here year 3 is ADP, which is exactly the rule we already have. Under this reading nothing about
keeper pricing changes at all and the Player Tax is a pure add-on.

## Why it matters more than anything else in the proposal
These two readings produce completely different leagues.

Reading 1: Jaxon Smith-Njigba, a 7th round pick who is now the 5th best player in football,
costs a 7th round pick every year forever. The entire penalty is $10 in year four.

Reading 2: JSN costs a 1st, same as today, and the $10 is just extra.

## What needs to happen
Chad confirms which one he meant before anything goes on a ballot. Everything else in the
proposal is clear and workable; this one line decides whether it is a small add-on or a rewrite
of the whole keeper economy.
