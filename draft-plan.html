The Round 3 Call

Take the RB

This is the recommendation. Nineteen RBs are already kept and every single RB inside ECR 40 is off the board. Only fifteen RBs with an ECR under 100 exist in the entire pool, and eleven other teams are shopping for them.

You have Etienne and Montgomery. Those are real but neither is a league-winner. Jeremiyah Love at ECR 41 is the top back on the board and a rookie, so he carries next-year value on top of this year's start.

Between #27 and your next pick at #46, eleven selections happen. Assume four or five of them are RBs. The tier does not survive the gap.

The case against

Nico Collins sits at ECR 13 and could still be there. That is a fourteen-slot value gap and by raw ECR it is the correct pick.

The reason to pass: you already start JSN and Rice. A third high-end WR wins you the value argument and still leaves you starting Montgomery as your RB2 in a league where everyone else kept their backs.

The one exception. If Collins, DeVonta Smith and Garrett Wilson are somehow all gone but Love and Kyren are both still sitting, you can take the WR that fell and grab a back at #46. Only do that if two of the top three RBs are still on the board.

Why Rookies Are Worth More Than Their ECR

"First keeper year costs the round you drafted him, every year after that costs his current expert consensus rank."
F The Raiders keeper rule, quoted from the live rule-vote page
That single sentence is the whole game. A player you draft in round 13 keeps at round 13 next season no matter what he becomes. The proof is sitting in this league already: Chris Drake took Drake Maye in the 13th round last year. Maye now ranks ECR 37 and Chris keeps him at a 13th. That one pick is +112 of surplus, the single biggest edge on the board, and it came from a throwaway pick nobody remembers.

Where the value actually lives

An early-round rookie is not a keeper bargain. Take Love at R3 and he keeps at R3, which is roughly what he is worth. You gained nothing on price.

The windfall only appears when the drafted round is far cheaper than what the player becomes. That means rounds 12 through 15. Your R13 at #147 is worth more as a lottery ticket than as a starter.

Play it deliberately

You hold four picks from R12 on: #142, #147, #166, #171. Spend at most one on a kicker and one on a defense, both of which you can stream.

That leaves two swings at a rookie back who could be a 13th-round keeper in 2027. Take the rookie with the clearest path to touches, not the best name.
